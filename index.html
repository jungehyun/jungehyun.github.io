Research

Mineral type in soil C models

Most global soil carbon models represent mineral protection by texture alone, yet soils of equal clay content but different minerals (short-range-order Fe and Al phases, 1:1 versus 2:1 clays) can hold carbon of different age. I build mineral type into a depth-resolved, process-based compartment model with particulate and mineral-associated pools and calibrate it against global compilations of stocks, POM/MAOM fractions, heterotrophic respiration and ISRaD radiocarbon. The aim: a model in which mineralogy, not texture alone, sets how long carbon persists.

Low-cost MRV for soil carbon

Soil-based carbon dioxide removal is only as credible as its measurement, reporting and verification (MRV), and MRV is what makes it expensive. I am developing a low-cost MRV system that works in the field (Sejong Science Fellowship, National Research Foundation of Korea, 2024–2028, PI), building on rapid measurement of soil carbon forms (thermal analysis, spectroscopy) and on field-calibrated process models that turn sparse samples into stock change with quantified uncertainty.

Soils shaped by people

I study soils that people have built or manage, such as urban green spaces and biochar-amended fields, to quantify the ecosystem services they deliver and how carbon and nitrogen cycle through them.
